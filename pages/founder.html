Founder story

From hawking to a fintech: the story behind ESSENCE and FlashPay

Adeshina Michael Oluwatoyin has been building things for as long as he can remember. As a boy he played with code and tried to build blogs. During his industrial training (SIWES) he sharpened those skills into web development and architecture.

Before there was a company, there was hard work. He has hawked goods on the street. He has worked shifts at a bakery and at a pure water factory. While still in school, he made and packaged his own products. That was when the idea of ESSENCE was born. He called it ESSENCE Foods and dreamed of growing it into a manufacturing company, with food, drinks, chemicals and more. That idea is not dead. It is only the beginning.

FlashPay came from another conviction. In his school days he wrote down an idea about making banking easier and left it for years. One day, going through his old jotters, he found it again.

By then he understood the problem from the inside. Working at the bakery, he handled cash every day and went to the bank to deposit and withdraw, queuing for hours. He kept asking: what if banking could be easier?

Then he watched OPay, PalmPay and Moniepoint arrive. He did not see competition. He saw an opportunity to learn. He studied them, learned from them, and started on his conviction.

He built it from an Android phone, with no laptop and no team. He often worked past midnight without proper sleep. Coding on a phone has real limits, and some bugs stayed unsolved for two weeks or more, when giving up felt like an option. He kept going. Along the way he used AI tools to speed up research and make the work easier.

In July 2026 he registered ESSENCE BUSINESS PLATFORM LIMITED in Ibafo, Ogun State. Today the company has three products: Vibee, a live social app; FlashPay, a mobile wallet app in testing with a waiting list open; and ADDY, a pickup-first online supermarket being built.

What building FlashPay has taught us

  1. An old idea is not a dead idea. Write down your convictions and come back to them when you are ready.
  2. Study the people ahead of you. Learn from the companies that came first, then build your own carefully.
  3. Start small and finish each piece. Every screen was built, tested and connected before the next began.
  4. Money software is judged on failure, not success. A failed payment should return the money, and an unclear one should stay pending until it is confirmed.
  5. Keep going when it gets hard. The bugs that nearly stopped us taught us the most.
